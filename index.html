AI in Energy Operations

A guide to the EU AI Act, one use case at a time

The EU AI Act is Europe's first law on artificial intelligence. Rather than regulating the technology itself, it asks what a system is used for and places it in one of a few categories. A small number of uses are banned outright. A larger group is treated as high risk and carries substantial obligations for the companies that build and deploy them. The great majority of uses carry very little. Because the category follows the use, the same technology can sit in different places depending on the job it is given. Most of the Act's rules apply from August 2026. This guide works through the AI systems now common in energy operations, one use case at a time, and explains which category each is likely to fall into, where the boundaries lie, and what follows in practice.

About this guide

This guide began as a personal research project on how the EU AI Act applies to the kinds of AI systems now appearing across energy operations. Each page takes one use case, describes what the technology does, points to the parts of the regulation most likely to be relevant, and notes the questions worth asking before deployment.

Everything here is compiled from public sources: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and its recitals, European Commission guidance, the GDPR together with EDPB and national supervisory authority guidance, and publicly announced industry AI programmes. The use cases reflect current trends in how AI is being used in the sector.

This is a guide and general information only. It does not constitute legal advice, and every real deployment needs its own assessment.
