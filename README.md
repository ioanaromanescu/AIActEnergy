# AI in Energy Operations: An EU AI Act Guide

**A plain-language guide to how the EU AI Act applies to the AI systems now in use across energy operations.**

**[→ Open the interactive version](https://ioanaromanescu.github.io/AIActEnergy/)** — a clickable risk map, the cards, and a short orientation tool.

---

The EU AI Act is Europe's first law on artificial intelligence. Rather than regulating the technology itself, it asks what a system is used for and places it in one of a few categories. A small number of uses are banned outright. A larger group is treated as high risk and carries substantial obligations for the companies that build and deploy them. The great majority of uses carry very little. Because the category follows the use, the same technology can sit in different places depending on the job it is given. Most of the Act's rules apply from August 2026.

This guide works through the AI systems now common in energy operations, one use case at a time, and explains which category each is likely to fall into, where the boundaries lie, and what follows in practice.

Compiled from public sources. The use cases reflect current trends in how AI is being used in the sector. This is a guide and general information, not legal advice.

## The map

Where each use case tends to sit, and how much the GDPR matters alongside.

| # | Use case | Where it tends to sit under the AI Act | GDPR weight |
|---|----------|----------------------------------------|-------------|
| 01 | Predictive maintenance | Minimal in the usual advisory setup; Annex I becomes relevant where the system acts on regulated machinery (2027) | Low |
| 02 | Inspection drones and robots | Minimal for asset inspection; EU drone rules run alongside as their own regime | Medium |
| 03 | GenAI assistants on documents | Article 50 transparency; general-purpose model duties sit upstream with the provider | Medium |
| 04 | Seismic and reservoir modelling | Minimal | Low |
| 05 | Corrosion and integrity detection | Minimal | Low |
| 06 | Methane and emissions MRV | Minimal; the EU Methane Regulation is the live instrument here | Low |
| 07 | Energy trading and forecasting | Outside the listed categories; market rules apply instead | Low |
| 08 | Drilling automation | Annex I becomes relevant as product rules phase in from 2027 | Low |
| 09 | Gas-grid and supply management | Annex III point 2 names critical infrastructure | Medium |
| 10 | Computer vision for site safety | Depends on whether outputs stay at the level of alerts or attach to individuals | High |
| 11 | Fatigue and drowsiness monitoring | Depends on system design; Recital 18 addresses physical states such as fatigue | High |
| 12 | Workforce screening and scheduling | Annex III point 4 names employment and worker management | High |

The pattern worth noticing: the Act follows the use, not the technology. The same computer vision that is unremarkable when it inspects a pipeline changes character the moment it is pointed at the people working next to it.
